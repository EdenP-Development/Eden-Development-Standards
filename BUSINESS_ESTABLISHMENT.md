# BUS-07 — Business establishment and reinvestment v1.0

Status: planning standard, 2026-11-25. No entity, tax election, account, assumed name, trademark, payment or spending authorization. Kentucky/US baseline; verify applicable rules and obtain qualified tax/legal advice before decisions.

## Structure
Eden Practical is the umbrella identity; Spudacus (including GridMates), EPS and EPD remain distinct brands. A single Kentucky LLC is a candidate, not an approved or registered entity. Compare sole proprietorship and LLC based on liability, ownership, tax, insurance, administrative burden, contracts and publisher identity. A single-member LLC normally defaults to disregarded federal income tax treatment unless elected otherwise; an LLC is not automatic tax savings or absolute liability protection. Confirm actual ownership, IP transfers/licenses, operating agreement and employment/conflict restrictions before formation. Check name availability, assumed names and trademark clearance separately. Kentucky sole proprietors using names without the owner's real name generally file assumed-name certificates with the county clerk; verify entity-specific rules.

## Dated cost and compliance snapshot
Kentucky LLC articles filing $40; annual report $15; registered agent required. Kentucky limited liability entity tax (LLET) generally has a $175 annual minimum for applicable entities, potentially even with no profit. Confirm applicability, exemptions and filing obligations with DOR/CPA; formation fees alone are not the cost of operating. Review annual report deadlines (generally June 30 following formation year and annually thereafter), state tax registration, local occupational licenses/taxes, home business rules, sales/use tax on software/digital products and marketplace facilitator treatment for each channel. Kentucky has no universal statewide business license; local and activity-specific rules may apply. EIN is free directly from IRS when needed; form entity first if applicable. FinCEN's August 11, 2026 final rule exempts US-created companies from BOI reporting; verify at formation. Google Play registration is separate; see BUS-05 and recheck fees. Do not assume storefront collection covers all tax obligations.

## Phases
Now: keep records of existing development income and costs, including merchandise or donations if applicable; establish actual business commencement and obligations with a professional rather than assuming beta status removes them. Preserve receipts, ownership and asset evidence privately; continue product development. Review applicable registrations before conducting business, not merely before first paid app.
Before commercial activity/expansion: determine legal structure and publisher, applicable registrations, tax accounts, insurance, business contact, separate banking and bookkeeping, payment processor terms and customer obligations. Owner approves each action. Do not transfer assets, change developer account identity or sign contracts automatically.
After formation if chosen: maintain separate finances, operating records, registered agent, filing calendar, tax estimates and renewals; document contributions/distributions. Review transfer of existing IP and store accounts carefully; preserve application signing and entitlement continuity.

## Financial records
Use a private ledger with date, product/brand, storefront, currency, gross sales, discounts/refunds, platform fees, taxes withheld/collected, payout, expense category, receipt, business purpose and reconciliation status. Distinguish revenue, cash receipts, owner contributions, transfers and distributions. Reconcile storefront reports to payouts and bank records monthly as practical; avoid double counting. Track business-use portion of mixed expenses and obtain advice on capitalization, deductions, startup costs, estimated income/self-employment taxes and state/local obligations. Do not assume all hardware or subscriptions are deductible. No personal financial details, tax IDs, statements or receipts in public repos. Retention follows applicable requirements and professional advice.

## Reinvestment priorities
1. Required taxes, refunds, chargebacks and compliance obligations.
2. Operating costs and a prudent reserve for support, security and maintenance.
3. Reliability, testing devices, backups, accessibility, hardware and necessary tools.
4. Product expansion, storefront assets and measured promotion.
5. Owner distributions or additional investment as approved.
No fixed percentages, debt commitments or revenue targets. Allocate only after actual available cash and obligations are reconciled. Evaluate incremental costs and customer value; do not scale recurring overhead on hoped-for revenue.
